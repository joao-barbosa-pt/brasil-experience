# E SE FÔSSEMOS? — BRASIL EXPERIENCE
## Master product, creative, UX, travel and technical specification

### 1. PROJECT IN ONE SENTENCE

Create a cinematic, interactive travel experience that starts in Porto and makes the visitor feel as if the Brazil trip is already happening — then lets the group explore, customize and ultimately decide the trip.

This is not a travel agency site.
It is not a slideshow.
It is not “Sofia’s trip”.

It is a shared journey, with the creator trying to convince Sofia, and with the possibility of friends joining later.

---

# 2. WHAT IS ALREADY DECIDED

## Identity

Working title:
**E SE FÔSSEMOS?**

Secondary framing:
**PORTO → BRASIL**

Possible supporting line:
**The elegance of yesterday. The freedom of today.**

The exact title/tagline can be refined later.

## Audience

Primary:
- creator
- Sofia

Potential:
- additional friends who receive the site/link

Therefore:
- never hardcode the experience around one person
- use Sofia’s name sparingly and naturally, only where useful
- do not label the product “Sofia’s Summer”
- the emotional voice can be “we”, “us”, “imagine this”, “what if we went?”

## Device priority

Mobile-first.

Desktop is a richer “cinematic mode”, not the source design that later gets squeezed into mobile.

## Initial duration strategy

Default proposal:
**10 days**

Creator’s ideal:
**12 days**

The duration choice should happen relatively late, after the visitor has seen enough of the experience to want the trip.

Supported choices:
**7 / 10 / 12 / 14 days**

Do not present 12 as mandatory.

# 4. OPENING CINEMATIC — PORTO TO RIO

Target duration:
**45–75 seconds**

This is the most important first build.

## Scene 01 — Porto

Black / deep blue.

Very subtle airport ambience.

Coordinates:
**PORTO**
`41.242° N · 8.678° W`

A camera slowly approaches an airport departures board.

## Scene 02 — Departure board

Create a stylized 3D board inspired by Porto Airport.

Do NOT spend time reconstructing the entire real airport.

The board should feel believable:
- rows
- flight times
- destination codes
- gate
- boarding status
- subtle screen/LED animation

Among ordinary European destinations, a special line appears:

**RIO DE JANEIRO — GIG**

Then:

**BOARDING**

Potentially:
**PORTO → RIO DE JANEIRO**

## Scene 03 — Airport

Camera leaves the board.

Simplified 3D terminal:
- architecture
- glass
- lights
- silhouettes
- luggage
- depth
- reflections
- atmospheric fog

It should feel like being inside an airport, not like looking at a 3D model.

## Scene 04 — Gate

Camera moves toward the gate.

The pace slows.

## Scene 05 — Airplane

Transition into the aircraft.

Seat.
Window.
Subtle cabin lighting.

## Scene 06 — Window

Camera approaches the window.

Clouds.

## Scene 07 — Atlantic

Clouds clear.

Ocean.

The colour palette slowly changes from cool Portuguese tones to warmer Brazilian light.

## Scene 08 — Sugarloaf reveal

Ocean.
Rio coastline.

Music changes.

**RIO DE JANEIRO**

Then:

# 11. BRANCHING INTERACTION

The visitor’s decisions can alter the trip.

Example:

## Helicopter?

YES:
- add helicopter experience
- add approximate cost
- show aerial golden-hour scene
- potentially alter budget

NO:
- show ground sunset alternative
- lower budget
- different recommendation

Other branches:
- more beach → more beach time
- more nature → Paraty/Ilha Grande weighting
- more nightlife → Rio/Búzios weighting
- budget lower → reduce premium stay/expensive experiences
- reject destination → recommend alternative

Important:
Use controlled branching. Do not create a combinatorial explosion of unique scenes.

- effort
- terrain
- heat exposure

- wildlife/fauna exposure

- safety guidance

- explain that fauna exposure varies by habitat/activity

- follow local guidance

# 31. QUALITY BAR

The final experience should feel like:
- a luxury editorial travel film
- a modern interactive story
- a beautifully designed private proposal
- a credible trip planner underneath

It should NOT feel like:
- a tourism board
- a booking site
- a dashboard
- a generic AI-generated landing page
- a Carnival cliché
- a 3D tech demo

Core principle:
**If a feature does not make the trip feel more real, beautiful, useful, or emotionally compelling, do not add it.**
